## Decisions & flags before print

1. **Grams vs millilitres** — the label template says "000ML" but the
   website, schema (250 GRM) and SKUs are all in grams. Recommendation:
   print **g** (250g / 500g) so packaging, website and structured data all
   match. If you'd rather sell by volume, change the site + schema too.
2. **"Patent-Backed Formula"** — the website trust bar claims this. Do
   **not** put it on packaging (and consider removing from the site) unless
   a patent/application number actually exists — it's a legally risky claim
   in print. The copy above leans on the safer, true claim: 10+ years
   commercial use.
3. **Cure time** — site FAQ says "overnight"; the original commercial spec
   in Notion says 48-hour cure. Label copy above says "24–48 hours to fully
   cure" to be safe. Align the site FAQ if you keep this.
4. **Durability wording** — Notion drafts mention "1+ year / 18+ month
   durability". Kept off the label: the decade-proven-formula claim is
   stronger and doesn't invite warranty arguments.
5. **Clear finish** — the formula dries clear (from the commercial spec).
   Worth adding "Dries clear" as a small call-out if space allows — it's a
   differentiator vs coloured competitors.
6. **Safety data sheet** — Vault notes flag SDS/compliance (R5–8k) as a
   launch cost. The safety paragraph above is label-level only; you still
   need the SDS done before retail (Outdoor Warehouse etc. will ask).
7. **Batch number & fill date** — legally sensible and retailers expect it;
   space reserved in the base strip.
